## ✅ MAIN POST — copy from here

This snake game took me under an hour.

Not a weekend. Not "a few evenings after work". One sitting, and most of that hour went into
deciding what I wanted, not typing it out.

It's called NEON SERPENT. There isn't a single image file in it and no sound files either.
Every sprite is drawn to a canvas in code, and every sound is generated by the Web Audio API
the moment you hear it. No React, no game engine, nothing installed from npm. It runs offline
and installs to your phone.

What's in it, since an hour sounds like it can't be much:

* 47 mutations you draft as you play. Some pairs combine into synergies. A few are curses
  that make you much stronger and permanently worse off, and those are the ones people pick.
* 20 snake skins, each one drawn by its own code. The dragon has horns and slit pupils, the
  ghost fades out toward its tail, the robot's visor narrows when it blinks.
* A coin shop, 106 achievements, XP, all saved on your device.
* Holds 60fps with a 90 segment snake on a mid-range phone.

Now the actual reason I'm posting this.

The game is the least interesting part. It's snake. What I keep thinking about is that an
hour of describing what I wanted produced something that would have cost me weeks not long
ago, in a language and a domain I don't work in daily.

And most of my work isn't games. It's the boring middle: [cleaning up messy spreadsheets,
writing the same boilerplate for the tenth time, reformatting reports for someone else's
template, digging through code I didn't write, documentation nobody will read]. That is
exactly the category where the cost has quietly collapsed.

The shift isn't that AI writes things for you. It's that the tedious 80 percent stops being
a reason not to start. Ideas that used to die at "that'll take me three days" now get built
the same afternoon.

It still needs you though. It went the wrong direction twice and I had to be blunt about what
"good" actually meant. At one point the play area sat at 320 pixels on a 1080p screen and
neither of us caught it for a while. If you can't tell good output from bad, this doesn't
help you much. If you can, it's the biggest change in how I work that I've seen in years.

Game's in the comments if you want to try it. It took an hour. That's the whole point.

## 📋 BEFORE YOU POST

1. Host it first. GitHub Pages, about 10 minutes. Post without a working link is wasted.
2. Record 15 to 25 seconds with `Win + Alt + R`. Start screen, a couple of bites with a combo
   running, a mutation draft, a synergy banner. Video reaches much further than text alone.
3. Video in the post, links in the first comment.
4. Tuesday to Thursday, 9 to 11am IST.
5. Stay free for an hour afterwards and reply to everyone. First hour decides the reach.
6. This angle gets more disagreement than a normal project post. That's good for reach, as
   long as you answer people properly instead of arguing.
